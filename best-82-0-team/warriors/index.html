Key takeaways

  • The Warriors have 6 starting-position pools, 4 at shooting guard and 2 at small forward, plus 4 sixth-man pools. There is no Warriors point guard, power forward or center pool.
  • Stephen Curry is never a point guard in 82-0. No Warriors point guard pool has more than three players. Draft him at shooting guard in the 2010s or 2020s, or as a sixth man (+4.17, the fourth-biggest bench boost in the game).
  • Even at 95%, Curry beats Klay Thompson at shooting guard by about 0.8 team power.
  • Rick Barry, Nate Thurmond and Derek Fisher never appear in a Warriors pool.
  • Draymond Green's 96 defense can't earn a big-man anchor bonus for the Warriors, because he only appears at small forward or as a sixth man.

Curry at shooting guard

Curry's Warriors point guard pools have two players in the 2010s (Curry and Jordan Poole) and three in the 2020s (Curry, Chris Paul and Poole), so the game never offers them, and Curry played for no other team. At shooting guard he counts at 95% and costs a chemistry point, but his 98 offense and 99 shooting still make him the strongest pick in both the 2010s and 2020s pools: about 0.8 team power ahead of Klay Thompson and 0.3 ahead of Chris Paul.

If Curry is already on your roster, the 2020s pool still has Chris Paul, who beats Klay by about 0.5 even as a secondary shooting guard.

Durant and the small forward pools

The 2010s small forward pool is Kevin Durant (97), Klay Thompson (secondary), Andre Iguodala and Draymond Green (secondary). Durant is the clear pick, about 1.6 team power ahead of Klay.

The 2020s pool has no Durant: Klay Thompson (secondary), Andre Iguodala, Draymond Green (secondary), Andrew Wiggins and Gary Payton II (secondary). Draymond is the best defender in either pool (96), but at small forward he trails Iguodala by about 0.6 team power, and the defensive anchor bonuses only count your power forward and center.

Old-school Warriors: the 1980s and 1990s guard pools

Before Curry, the Warriors' pools are shooting guard pools full of forwards and point guards playing out of position. The 1980s pool is Chris Mullin, Bernard King, Mitch Richmond and Tim Hardaway, and the 1990s pool is Mullin, Mark Price, Richmond, Hardaway and Latrell Sprewell. In both, Richmond is the only natural shooting guard, and he and Mullin (secondary) are virtually even for team power.

Shooting is the reason to take one of these older pools: Mark Price (94) and Chris Mullin (92) both have the 90+ shooting for the +4 Elite Perimeter Threat bonus.

Warriors you can't draft

Rick Barry (92) is in the database as a Warrior, but every 1960s and 1970s Warriors pool is too small to appear, so the game never offers him. The same goes for Nate Thurmond (89), and Derek Fisher's 2000s Warriors pools are too small too. Chris Webber, Robert Parish and Jermaine O'Neal appear only as Warriors sixth men.

With no point guard, power forward or center pool, there is no all-Warriors lineup in 82-0. A Warriors spin gives you one shooter or wing; plan your bigs and your defense around it.
