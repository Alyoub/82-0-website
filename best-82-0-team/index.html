Why some legends are hard to find

A scouting report only appears when that team and era has at least four players who can play the position you are drafting. Very few pools pass that test at point guard and center:

That is why some legends only turn up at a secondary position or as a sixth man:

  • Magic Johnson can be drafted as a small forward in 1970s and 1990s Lakers reports and as a sixth man in 1970s, 1980s and 1990s Lakers reports, but never as a point guard.
  • Michael Jordan can be drafted as a small forward in 1990s Bulls reports and as a sixth man in 1980s and 1990s Bulls reports, but never as a shooting guard.
  • Bill Russell can be drafted as a sixth man in 1960s Celtics reports, but never as a center.

How to draft a team like this

  1. Fill point guard and center when a deep report shows up. They have the fewest pools.
  2. Favor two-way players. Offense (25% of team power) and defense (20%) carry the most weight.
  3. Chase chemistry: an elite playmaker (88+), a 90+ shooter, a center who defends at 88+, team averages of 75+ and four different roles.
  4. Prefer natural positions. Each secondary position costs 5% of a player's overall and 1 point of chemistry.
  5. Take a sixth man who scores and covers three positions.
  6. Coach steady once you are the favorite: Balance the minutes, Lock down and Protect their legs.
