Key takeaways

  • The Lakers have 17 starting-position pools (2 point guard, 3 shooting guard, 4 small forward, 6 power forward, 2 center) and 6 sixth-man pools. No other team has more than 10 starting-position pools.
  • Wilt Chamberlain and Kareem Abdul-Jabbar never appear in a Lakers center pool. You can draft them only at power forward or as a sixth man.
  • In the 2010s center pool, Anthony Davis is the stronger pick than natural center Dwight Howard by about 1.5 team power.
  • LeBron James (99) appears in eight Lakers pools, and as a sixth man he gives the biggest bench boost in the game (+4.42).
  • Built only from Lakers pools, the best five averages about 77 wins and goes 82-0 about 1 in 128 seasons with the best coaching calls. It is a what-if: a real draft gives you one Lakers pick at most.

Lakers pool counts and how reports are chosen

These counts describe the eligible opening-state pool inventory, not a fixed chance that the Lakers appear among the three reports. The first report is drawn from a uniform shuffle of eligible team-era pools, but the next two must use different teams. Later rounds prefer teams you have not opened, and drafted players are removed before pools are rebuilt.

Why Wilt and Kareem never play center

At the opening state, the four-player threshold excludes the Lakers' other center eras. The 1960s pool has 1 player (Wilt), the 1970s pool has 2 (Wilt and Kareem), the 1980s pool has 3 (Kareem, Bob McAdoo and Spencer Haywood) and the 1990s pool has 2 (Shaquille O'Neal and Dennis Rodman). The 2020s pool also stops at 3 (Anthony Davis, Dwight Howard and Marc Gasol).

That is why Wilt (98) can only be drafted as a 1970s power forward or sixth man, and Kareem (97) as a 1970s or 1980s power forward or sixth man. At power forward they count at 95% and cost a chemistry point. In the 1970s power forward pool, Kareem is actually the slightly stronger pick, by about 0.3 team power. His defense (90 to 88), shooting (72 to 60) and playmaking (75 to 72) outweigh Wilt's edge in offense and rebounding.

Shaquille O'Neal is the exception. The 2000s Lakers center pool has exactly four players (Shaq, Karl Malone, Pau Gasol and Horace Grant), so Shaq plays his natural position there. In the 2010s center pool, Anthony Davis beats Dwight Howard by about 1.5 team power even as a secondary center: his offense (90 to 82) and shooting (78 to 42) matter far more than the 5% discount. Shaq (88), Davis (95) and Howard (95) all have the 88+ defense that earns the +4 Lockdown Anchor chemistry bonus at center.

Where to use LeBron James and Luka Doncic

LeBron James appears in more Lakers pools than anyone: point guard, small forward and power forward in both the 2010s and 2020s, plus both decades' sixth-man pools. Small forward is his natural spot, so he keeps his full 99 there; at point guard and power forward he counts at 95%.

  • 2010s point guard: LeBron is about 1.4 team power stronger than Steve Nash. Nash still has a use: with 92 shooting he is the only Lakers starter option rated 90+ in shooting, which earns the +4 Elite Perimeter Threat bonus. (Jerry West also has 90 shooting, but he only appears as a sixth man, and that bonus counts starters only.)
  • 2020s point guard: even at 95%, LeBron edges natural point guard Luka Doncic by about 0.4. Luka can also be drafted at shooting guard and small forward from the same decade.
  • 2010s small forward: LeBron leads Kobe Bryant, who plays small forward as a secondary position, by about 0.6.
  • Sixth man: LeBron adds +4.42 team power off the bench, the biggest boost of any player in the game. Luka (+4.24) ranks third. Landing the 2010s or 2020s Lakers on your sixth-man spin is the best case for a strong starting five.

Lakers draft tips

  • Hoping for a center? The Lakers own 2 of the 9 eligible opening-state center pools. If either report appears, take Shaq from the 2000s pool or Anthony Davis from the 2010s pool.
  • 1970s small forward pool: it is Magic Johnson (as a secondary position), Elgin Baylor, Connie Hawkins and Lou Hudson. Magic's 99 playmaking earns the +5 Elite Playmaker bonus if no one else in your five has 88+ playmaking.
  • Plan around LeBron: if you already drafted him from the Cavaliers or Heat, the 2010s Lakers point guard pool disappears for the rest of that draft.
