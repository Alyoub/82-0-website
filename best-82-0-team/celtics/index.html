Key takeaways

  • The Celtics have 10 starting-position pools, second only to the Lakers, and 7 sixth-man pools, the most of any team.
  • Larry Bird can never be drafted at small forward. Every Celtics small forward pool from his decades has three players or fewer, so he appears only as a 1980s power forward or a 1970s–1990s sixth man.
  • The 2010s are the only Celtics decade with a pool at all five positions. Their shooting guard pool (8 players) and sixth-man pool (14 players) are the largest of their kind in the game.
  • In the 2010s center pool, Kevin Garnett is the stronger pick than Shaquille O'Neal by about 0.9 team power, even as a secondary center.
  • Bill Russell, Bob Cousy, Sam Jones and John Havlicek appear only as sixth men.

Celtics pool counts and how reports are chosen

These counts describe the eligible opening-state pool inventory, not a fixed chance that Boston appears among the three reports. The first report is drawn from a uniform shuffle of eligible team-era pools, but the next two must use different teams. Later rounds prefer teams you have not opened, and drafted players are removed before pools are rebuilt.

The Larry Bird question

Bird is one of the best players in 82-0, with 97 overall, 94 shooting and 92 playmaking. For Boston, though, the game offers him only in the 1980s power forward pool and in the 1970s, 1980s and 1990s sixth-man pools. The Celtics' small forward pools from those decades have 2, 1 and 3 players, so none of them ever appears.

At power forward Bird counts at 95% and costs a chemistry point, and he is still about 1.2 team power stronger than Kevin McHale, the natural power forward in the same pool. He can also bring two chemistry bonuses on his own: +5 for an Elite Playmaker (88+ playmaking) and +4 for an Elite Perimeter Threat (90+ shooting), if no one else in your five already earns them.

As a sixth man he adds +3.96, the best bench boost in all three of those Celtics sixth-man pools. Whether the 1980s Celtics come up at power forward or on your sixth-man spin, Bird is the pick.

Choosing a Celtics big man

1980s center pool: Bill Walton (92), Kevin McHale (92, secondary), Dave Cowens (89), Robert Parish (89) and Artis Gilmore (88). Walton, Cowens, Parish and Gilmore all have 88+ defense, so any of them earns the +4 Lockdown Anchor bonus at center; McHale (86) does not. Walton is the strongest choice, about 0.2 team power ahead of Cowens and 0.6 ahead of Parish, thanks to his 94 defense and 85 playmaking.

2010s center pool: Shaquille O'Neal (97) played his last season in Boston, so he is here, alongside Kevin Garnett (95, secondary), Jermaine O'Neal, Rasheed Wallace and Al Horford. Garnett is the better center by about 0.9 team power: his 96 defense, 78 shooting and 82 playmaking outweigh Shaq's higher overall and offense. At power forward, where Garnett is natural and Shaq is not, the gap grows to about 1.5.

Ray Allen and the shooters

Ray Allen's 98 shooting is the best of any Celtic. He appears at shooting guard in the 2000s and 2010s, at small forward in the 2010s (as a secondary position), and he gives the best bench boost in both the 2000s and 2010s sixth-man pools (+3.78).

At shooting guard, Allen and Paul Pierce (secondary) are almost exactly even for team power, and Allen edges Kyrie Irving by about 0.1. The tiebreaker is chemistry: of the three, only Allen has the 90+ shooting that earns the +4 Elite Perimeter Threat bonus.
