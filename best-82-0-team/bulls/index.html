Best Bulls 82-0 Team

The Bulls are a strange case in 82-0. Michael Jordan shares the highest rating in the game (99), but Chicago has only four starting-position pools and none at point guard or center. Knowing where Jordan and his teammates actually appear is most of the strategy.

Key takeaways

  • The Bulls have 4 starting-position pools (2010s shooting guard, 1990s and 2010s small forward, 1990s power forward) and 5 sixth-man pools. There is no Bulls point guard or center pool.
  • Michael Jordan is never a shooting guard in 82-0. The 1980s and 1990s Bulls shooting guard pools have only three players each. He appears as a 1990s Bulls small forward, or as a sixth man for the 1980s and 1990s Bulls and the 2000s Wizards.
  • At small forward, Jordan is still about 0.55 team power stronger than Scottie Pippen, who plays the position naturally.
  • In the 1990s power forward pool, Pippen is the strongest pick and Dennis Rodman the weakest: Horace Grant beats Rodman by about 1.5 team power.
  • Derrick Rose can't be drafted at point guard for Chicago, and Nate Thurmond can't be drafted as a Bull at all.

Why Jordan plays small forward here

Jordan was a shooting guard, but the 1980s Bulls shooting guard pool is just Jordan, Pippen and George Gervin, and the 1990s pool is Jordan, Pippen and Toni Kukoc. Both are one player short of the four the game needs. The 1990s small forward pool does qualify: Jordan (as a secondary position), Pippen, Dennis Rodman (secondary) and Kukoc.

Playing small forward costs Jordan 5% of his overall rating and a chemistry point, and he is still the better pick over Pippen by about 0.55 team power. One thing narrows the gap: Pippen's 88 playmaking earns the +5 Elite Playmaker bonus (worth 0.4 team power) if no one else in your five has 88+ playmaking. Jordan's 85 does not. Even then, Jordan stays slightly ahead.

As a sixth man, Jordan adds +4.13, the fifth-biggest bench boost in the game. That makes the 1980s and 1990s Bulls a great sixth-man spin.

The 1990s power forward pool

The 1990s Bulls power forward pool has five players: Scottie Pippen (as a secondary position), Robert Parish (secondary), Dennis Rodman, Horace Grant and Toni Kukoc (secondary).

Rodman has the best defense (97) and rebounding (99) in the pool, but his 55 offense and 40 shooting make him the weakest all-around pick. Horace Grant is about 1.5 team power stronger, and Pippen is the strongest of all, about 1.4 ahead of Grant.

Pippen, Rodman, Grant and Parish all have 88+ defense, so any of them at power forward earns the +3 Interior Defensive Anchor bonus. That bonus only applies when your center does not already earn the +4 center version.

No point guard, no center

Every Bulls point guard pool and every Bulls center pool has three players or fewer. Derrick Rose (88) is a natural point guard, but the 2010s Bulls point guard pool is just Dwyane Wade, Rose and Rajon Rondo. You can only draft Rose as a 2010s shooting guard or as a sixth man.

The 2010s shooting guard pool is the biggest Bulls starting pool: Dwyane Wade (95), Jimmy Butler (91, secondary), Derrick Rose (88, secondary), Zach LaVine (87), Rip Hamilton (86) and Rajon Rondo (85, secondary). Wade is the natural pick, about 0.55 team power ahead of Butler.

Nate Thurmond (89) is in the database as a Bull but never appears in a Bulls pool, because every 1970s Bulls pool is too small. Artis Gilmore, Ben Wallace, Joakim Noah, Nikola Vucevic, Pau Gasol, George Gervin and DeMar DeRozan appear only in Bulls sixth-man pools.

Because the Bulls can't fill point guard or center, there is no all-Bulls lineup in 82-0. Treat a Bulls spin as one strong piece: a wing (Jordan, Pippen, Wade or Butler) or a 1990s power forward.
