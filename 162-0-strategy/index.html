Key takeaways

  • Even the strongest nine wins about 95.6% of its games (about 155 wins), and goes 162-0 in about 1 in 450 seasons with the best calls.
  • A typical drafted nine wins about 143 games, but its chance of 162-0 is about 1 in 620 million.
  • Pitching is the biggest single rating: 65% of your run prevention comes from one player.
  • Keep average stamina at 80+ or your team fades as the season goes on.

How the 162-0 draft works

  • Nine spots: pitcher, catcher, first base, second base, third base, shortstop and the three outfield spots.
  • Every spin lands on one of 29 clubs and one of four eras: Golden Age (1950–1969), Expansion (1970–1989), Power Era (1990–2009) and Modern Game (2010–2025). Clubs you have not used yet come up first.
  • Pick a player and place him. A natural position keeps 100% of his ratings, a secondary position 95%, a related role 85% (infield to infield, outfield to outfield) and an unfamiliar role 65%.
  • Specialists: only pitchers pitch, and only players who list catcher can catch.
  • One respin per draft. All 1,044 players and 29 clubs are original fictional creations.

Stamina and fatigue

If your nine's average stamina is under 80, your team loses strength as the season goes on: by game 162 you lose 0.12 points on both sides for every stamina point under 80. A team with 70 stamina ends the year 1.2 points weaker at the plate and in the field.

The September calls change this: Ride the regulars more than doubles fatigue, while Rest the regulars erases it completely.
