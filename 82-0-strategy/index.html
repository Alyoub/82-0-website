How to Go 82-0: The Complete Strategy Guide

Going 82-0 takes more than drafting the highest ratings. This guide breaks down exactly how the 82-0 game scores your team — ratings, chemistry, position fit, the sixth man and the three coaching calls — with numbers from 20,000 simulated seasons.

Key takeaways

  • No game is ever better than a 97% win chance, so even a flawless roster goes 82-0 in at most about 8% of seasons.
  • One of the strongest possible rosters went 82-0 about 1 time in 100 with steady coaching, and almost never with all-in coaching.
  • Offense is the biggest single rating (25% of team power), but chemistry and the sixth man can add up to 13 more points.
  • Once your team is the favorite, choose low-risk coaching calls. Risky calls help underdogs, not contenders.

Why 82-0 is so hard

Every game in the 82-0 challenge compares your team power with an opponent's. Opponents average 78 and range from 62 to 97, and the bigger your edge, the higher your chance to win. That chance is capped at 97%, so no game is ever a lock.

To finish 82 and 0 you have to win all 82 of those coin tosses in a row. Small differences in your per-game win chance turn into huge differences in your odds of a perfect season:

Per-game win chance vs. chance of going 82-0

In practice your team faces some strong opponents, so its average win chance sits well below the 97% cap. That is why every point of team power matters.

What team power is made of

Your five starters' ratings are averaged, weighted and combined into a single team power number. The sixth man is not part of the averages; he adds a separate bench boost.

What this means: a lineup of pure scorers looks great on offense but leaves a fifth of your power on the table if nobody defends. Two-way players are worth more than their overall rating suggests.

Chemistry, the hidden stat

Chemistry starts at 70 and ends up anywhere from 40 to 99. It is decided entirely by how your starters fit together.

Chemistry boosts

  • An elite playmaker (playmaking 88+): +5
  • An elite shooter (shooting 90+): +4
  • A center with defense 88+: +4 (or a power forward with defense 88+: +3)
  • Team averages of 75+ in offense, defense, rebounding and shooting: +5
  • Four or more different player roles (archetypes): +3

Chemistry drags

  • Each starter playing a secondary position: −1
  • Each starter far out of position: −3
  • No starter with defense 80+: −4
  • Fewer than two starters with shooting 80+: −3
  • Three or more starters with the same role: −2

Raising chemistry from 60 to 95 is worth about 2.7 extra wins per season for a strong team, all without changing a single overall rating.

Position fit

A player at his natural position counts at 100% of his overall rating. A listed secondary position counts at 95%, and playing far out of position drops to 85%. Fit only scales the overall rating, but every starter who is not at his natural position also costs chemistry.

Tip: before you spin, you can tap any open spot in your lineup to choose which position to draft next. Fill the position with the deepest pool early and keep your options open for the rest.

Choosing your sixth man

After your five starters, you spin once more for a sixth man of any position. He is scored on three things:

  • Scoring: 45% offense, 35% shooting and 20% overall.
  • Coverage: grows with every position he can play.
  • Fatigue relief: overall rating and athleticism, plus positions covered.

Together they create a bench boost between 0.75 and 5 points of team power. Going from the weakest to the strongest bench boost is worth about 4 wins over a season. A scorer who can play three positions earns roughly one more point of team power than an identical player who plays only one.

The three coaching calls

After games 20, 41 and 61 the season pauses and you choose a plan for the next stretch. Each call adds team power, but each also changes volatility: how much every game drifts toward a coin flip.

The rule: volatility helps underdogs and hurts favorites. If your team wins most of its games, a low-risk call makes those wins even more reliable. A high-risk call adds raw power but pulls every game toward 50/50, and for a good team that trade is a bad one.

How to build the best 82-0 team

  1. Land at least one elite playmaker (playmaking 88+).
  2. Draft two or more shooters rated 80+, ideally one at 90+.
  3. Anchor the paint with a center who defends at 88+.
  4. Keep team averages at 75+ in offense, defense, rebounding and shooting.
  5. Mix roles: aim for four different archetypes, never three of the same.
  6. Prefer natural positions, and use the lineup to choose which spot to spin next.
  7. Pick a sixth man who scores and covers several positions.
  8. Save your one Respin for a genuinely thin pool.
  9. Once you are the favorite, coach conservatively.
